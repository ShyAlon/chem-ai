## How stirring changes dissolving time

**Question.** Does sugar disappear from view faster when the water is stirred?

**Materials and setup.** Two clear cups, equal amounts of room-temperature water and granulated sugar, a spoon, and a timer. Use the same water volume and amount of sugar in each cup.

**Method.** Add sugar to both cups and start the timer. Stir one cup and leave the other still. Record when visible grains disappear from each cup, or record what remains at a fixed time. Rinse the cups afterward.

**Expected observation.** The stirred cup should lose its visible grains sooner. Stirring moves dissolved sugar away from the grain surfaces, allowing more sugar to dissolve. The activity compares dissolving rates.

## Which grains dissolve in water

**Question.** What happens when table salt and washed play sand are stirred into separate cups of water?

**Materials and setup.** Two clear cups, equal amounts of room-temperature water, small equal spoonfuls of table salt and washed play sand, and separate spoons.

**Method.** Put one material in each cup and stir each for the same amount of time. Let both cups sit, then compare what remains visible. Discard the sand in the trash during cleanup.

**Expected observation.** A small amount of salt dissolves in water and its grains disappear from view. Most sand grains remain visible and settle. The salt is still present in the clear solution.

## Reference-measurement comparison

The Data lab includes a water-density comparison. Measure water mass after subtracting the container mass and measure the water volume at a listed temperature. The backend computes density in g/mL and signed relative difference from the selected USGS reference. Initial temperatures are 10, 21, and 26.7 °C. The measurement reference is retrieved through the MCP data service, and the activity timeline shows retrieval and calculation. The result includes the source and measurement caveats.
